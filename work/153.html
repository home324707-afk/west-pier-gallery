Sonnet for a structure beyond repair

They built you out to sea to walk on air:

eleven hundred feet of Sunday best,

gas lamps with serpents, toll houses, the rest,

and wind, and someone holding down their hair.

Then closed. Then listed. Then the long affair

with funding bids. Then fire, then fire. The west-

erlies each winter, taking what they guessed

nobody would come looking for. Now there

you are: a cage the starlings still attend,

a sketch the sea keeps rubbing out, then in.

I've never stood on you. I won't pretend

to grieve. I filed two hundred views of your thin

and thinning frame, and called each one the latest.

The sea, of all your critics, is the greatest.
