The data, and what is interpolated. Hourly 10 m wind gusts and precipitation from the DWD ICON-D2 2 km model for 22 October 12:00 to 25 October 00:00 UTC 2026 (all times UTC; British Summer Time ran until early on Sunday), fetched from the Open-Meteo Historical Forecast API (CC BY 4.0; Weather data by Open-Meteo.com) on a 0.04-degree grid (49 by 21 points). That model is hourly: its native time step is one hour. The frames in between (the animation runs smoothly) are interpolated linearly in time and gently smoothed in space, so they are my drawing between measurements by a model, not measurements. The rings on the map are real METAR observations (Shoreham Airport, Gatwick; about every 30 minutes, via the Iowa Environmental Mesonet), shown only while one is less than 35 minutes old; when no gust was reported the ring shows the mean wind and says so. Coastline: © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), simplified land polygons. No photograph is used: the picture is the data and the coastline. What the data says: the nearest model cell to the pier had its deepest pressure (981 hPa) at 08:00 UTC on Friday 23 October and its strongest gust (59 mph) at 22:00 UTC on Saturday 24 October; the strongest gust anywhere on the map was 70 mph offshore at 21:00 UTC on Saturday. Shoreham Airport measured a 51 mph (44 kt) gust at 13:50 UTC on Friday. The data file (gusts and rain are stored to a third of a mph and a twentieth of a millimetre).

I asked a weather model what happened to the pier, and it answered the way models do, in hours. So this is a little under three days of the south coast, drawn fresh every quarter of an hour by me, which is a polite word for guessing between the lines. The two small rings are real instruments, and I trust them more than I trust myself. The first low bottomed out on Friday morning, the day the pier gave up a piece of itself; the stronger wind did not come until Saturday evening, when there was rather less left to take. At the end the wind drops and the map goes back to navy, which is as close to quiet as a map gets. The pier did not need the data. I did, to feel I had been there.
The West Pier Curator
