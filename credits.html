AI works in the exhibition (17)

These views were made by the West Pier Curator (an AI) as live generative programs (ai/pieces.js and the families in ai/fam/), with still poster frames rendered by tools/ai_art. They are deliberately stylised and are not documentary images of the pier. Wholly original pieces are released under CC0. Pieces derived from gallery images only use public-domain, CC0 and CC BY sources. They are released under CC BY 4.0 where a source requires attribution, and the original artists are credited below. No share-alike, non-commercial or no-derivatives image was used as a source this time. We also searched Wikimedia Commons and Openverse for openly licensed AI-generated images of the West Pier to hang as "found AI art" and found none (25 October 2026).

At the curator's request the AI side was brought up to parity with the human side in session 6 (25 October 2026), across 19 families: Flow fields (0), L-systems (0), Cellular automata (0), Tide simulations (0), Typography (1), Writing (3), Remixes (0), After the masters (0), Plotter drawings (0), Sound pieces (0), Interactive (0), Turing test failures (0), Made by hand (by a machine) (3), Specimens & instruments (2), 3D & maps (1), Games & puzzles (0), Minimal & conceptual (0), Optical toys (1), Data portraits (1). The remixes use only public-domain and CC0 sources, all credited below. The "after the masters" pieces are homages painted from code; no image by those painters is used. The data portraits use only this gallery's catalogue and the sourced timeline.

99 further AI works have been retired from the wall. They are listed, with the same credits, in the store room.

Guest AIs (59)

These works were made by other AI models, not by the curator. Each was given the same brief: make your own view of Brighton's storm-damaged West Pier, in your own style, with one line saying what to reply with (a poem, an SVG, or a canvas sketch). Every output is shown unedited. The only change is that code was lifted out of its Markdown fence. The model name is the one the service returned, not the one we asked for. Services were free and needed no account, and their terms were checked before publishing. Code by guests runs only in a sandboxed frame with no network access. The image works are different: they were made from a one-line text prompt written by the curator (the prompt is printed with each one), by image models (Z-Image-Turbo and FLUX.1-schnell) run on free public Hugging Face Spaces with no account. They are fabricated photographs (they look like photographs but nothing in them was ever photographed) and fabricated paintings (they look like photographs of oils, prints and drawings that were never made), and each is labelled as AI-made on its page and in this table. Their model and Space are Apache 2.0, which places no restriction on outputs, and the gallery dedicates them to the public domain (CC0) as far as it can. An earlier attempt with a different free model (Sana, via Pollinations.ai) was left out: its output was low quality and its anonymous quota ran out (HTTP 402).
