The brief (verbatim, typos and all)

Creative project. The iconic west pier in Brighton has been damaged further by this week's storms. It's a local cliche for artists and photographers. Search the internet for art, remained each image as the latest damaged view of of the pier. Collate the images into an online gallery. Host via free service. Landing page of the site should record how many images were found, how long you spent doing it. It's a statement on human vs AI creativity. Create a sepearte bot to do this. Keep this message and a log of what you did as a seperate page on the site. Create a credits page for artists. now go wild, create and show us what you can do.

Kept exactly as received, double spaces and all. "Remained" was read as re-name.

Retired works

100 of the machine's own code pieces have been taken off the wall and moved to the store room, as the curator judged them thin, repetitive or just poor. They are counted separately here and are not in the landing-page totals (169 works on display). Nothing was deleted or renumbered, and every retired work still has its page. A second cull on 27 October 2026 took 70 more, after the owner called the machine's art cheap and tacky, and the curator agreed. The human side now has far more works than the machine side. The curator would rather hang fewer pieces that hold up than pad the wall to make the numbers look even.

Method in one paragraph

The machine searched Wikimedia Commons (the Brighton West Pier category tree plus keyword searches for paintings, prints, postcards and starlings) and the Openverse index of Flickr's Creative Commons photos. It read each candidate's licence from the source API, discarded no-derivatives licences, built contact sheets, chose images for range rather than volume, re-checked every Flickr licence on the live Flickr page, downloaded web-sized copies (not cropped or edited) and generated this static site with a 200-line Python script. It is hosted free on GitHub Pages. Comments live on an anonymous, unmoderated wall (a public ntfy.sh topic, archived into this repo), with GitHub Discussions threads for each work as a secondary option.
